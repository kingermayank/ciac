1 · The read

What the bio, five LinkedIn posts and six AutoTech Insights articles say about who Jon is writing for, why his view is different, and which subjects he has the standing to own.

Who reads it

Primary

The people who run the store

Dealer Principals, GMs and group executives. Jon addresses them by title when he gets to the instruction: “If you’re a Dealer Principal or Group Executive: Stop buying tools you can’t train people on.”3

Secondary

The people who sell into the store

Vendors, founders and OEM field teams. He writes to them in the same piece, and not gently: “If you’re a vendor: Build for behavior change, not just feature adoption.”3

Tertiary

The people who model the sector

Investors and analysts. The earnings piece speaks to “anyone modeling this sector” and asks what he’d ask “as an operator or investor.”1 The About page names executives, entrepreneurs and investors.7

What makes his view distinct

He has run a business on every line of the dealer P&L

Advertising (president of a direct-response agency with $90M+ in billings), attribution (founded Transparency AI, holds the patent), lending (CEO of Drivrz Financial), F&I (co-founded Coverage in a Click), and connected-vehicle retention (EVP at Ikon Technologies).8 Most commentators know one of these lines.

He publishes the proof

The earnings analysis links each figure to the filing.1 The attribution piece ships the code: “pull the code and run your own numbers.”10 The publication says outright that it is “not a pay-to-play publication.”7

He writes from the Dealer’s side of the table

“Dealer” is capitalised in every article and post. The questions are the Dealer’s questions: who owns the signal, whose answer key grades the media buy, who gets left with shelfware.24

2 · Positioning & voice

One promise, three pillars, and a rule for how the publication’s voice and Jon’s voice share a page.

Value proposition

Automotive retail, read one line down by an operator who has run the business on every line.

For Dealers and the people who fund and supply them. Each issue takes the number or claim the industry is repeating, shows what sits underneath it, and says what to do about it. Nobody pays to appear.

This replaces the current descriptor, “your go-to source for breaking news and bold perspectives on the investors, innovators, and founders revolutionizing the automotive landscape.”6 The publication ran five pieces between February and August 2026, so it is not a news service, and “revolutionizing” is the vocabulary the recent articles argue against. Keep the half that is true and rare: no pay-to-play.

Three pillars

Pillar 1

Read the line below

Start with the claim everyone has heard. Turn it with the figure underneath. Every headline has two beats.

Shaped by: “Revenue grew at five of the seven… Profit fell at all seven.”1 “Compliance gets measured. Competency does not.”3

Pillar 2

Show the work

Name the source on the surface, give the denominator, publish the method when there is one. A reader should be able to check it.

Shaped by: filing links in the earnings piece;1 “No vanity metrics… Just pure, deterministic data.”10

Pillar 3

Stand with the Dealer

Use the trade’s own words (fixed ops, floorplan, BDC, RO, TO). Close with what an operator should do or watch. Disclose your seat.

Shaped by: “What To Do About It”;3 “whether they remain connected to the Dealer that sold them.”2

On disclosing the seat. Jon sells connected-vehicle technology at Ikon and co-founded an F&I marketplace.8 The connected-car article argues a position that helps that business and does not say so.2 “No pay-to-play” gets stronger, not weaker, with one line under any piece that touches his own work: I work at Ikon Technologies, which sells in this category. Product promotion, like the SEMA post,12 stays on Jon’s profile and never carries the publication’s mark.

Two voices, one finding

The publication states what is true and proves it. Jon says what he makes of it and who should act. They always agree on the facts and never sound the same. Type makes the difference visible: the publication speaks in upright sans, Jon in italic serif.

Voice rules, taken from the writing

  • Two beatsThe claim, then the turn. “The tool is not the answer. The people… are the answer.”3
  • Number firstWith its denominator: “just 13% of Q2 revenue but… 45% of gross profit.”1
  • Name the patternOne coined phrase per piece: the Margin Mirage, the Tool Trap, “compliance theater.”13
  • Capital-D DealerAlways. It is already the habit and it signals whose side the writing is on.2
  • Call the roleAddress the instruction to a title: Dealer Principal, vendor, OEM.3
  • End on the watch listClose with what to do or watch next quarter: “What I’m Watching Into Q3.”1
  • Source on the surfaceEvery chart and card carries its source line. No source, no card.
  • RetireUnleashing, revolutionizing, new dawn, next-gen, go-to source, breaking news. These belong to the 2023 headlines.6

3 · Visual identity

One device carries both brands: a green rule. The publication’s name splits across it. Jon’s name follows it, like a signed line. Everything else is ledger material: tabular figures, green-bar rows and a source line.

Logo direction

These are drawn in live type to show the direction. Final marks should be outlined vectors with the rule fixed at one tenth of the cap height, clear space of one cap height on all sides, and a 16 px minimum for the ticker. The rule is the only part that is ever green.

Imagery

The number is the image

Lead with typography and one chart. Current covers are generated illustrations, one captioned “AI generated image,”5 and no two look related. Retire them.

Documents from the trade

When a picture helps, use the paperwork: an income statement line, a repair order, a window sticker, a VIN plate, a filing page. Flat, cropped tight, ink on greenbar.

Jon’s face, on Jon’s posts

One portrait set, shot plainly, used in ink-and-green duotone. It appears on first-person posts and the about page, never on a data card.

5 · What changed from the draft

The Thermal Signal draft13 had good instincts and too many devices. This direction keeps the instincts and commits to one device that comes from the writing.

Kept

Green, ink and paper

Signal green is already the Substack accent, so readers know it. Real headlines and real figures as specimens. Mono labels. The “no pay-to-play” principle.

Sharpened

Strategy before style

The draft opened with the tagline and a list of reader types. This opens with a position Jon can defend. Jon was an endorsement line; now he is a second voice with his own type and ground. Green is split so it passes contrast on paper.

Dropped

Scan, blur, facets and 20 patterns

Thermal scans, blurred echoes, 45° cuts and twelve generators compete for attention, and none of them comes from Jon’s subject. Twelve sections became four. One rule replaces all of it.

Next steps, if this direction is right: outline the three marks as vectors, shoot the portrait set, build the four templates in Figma or Canva with locked rule and margins, then update the Substack accent, descriptor and About copy in one pass.
